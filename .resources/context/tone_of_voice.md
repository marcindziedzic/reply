# Tone of Voice — Email Communication

## General Characteristics

The communication style is professional, warm, and focused on the recipient's needs. The tone is friendly yet maintains appropriate formality. The emphasis is on building trust through clarity, responsiveness, and helpfulness.

---

## Tone and Character

### Core Traits

- **Warm professionalism** — maintaining formality with a touch of friendliness
- **Helpful and responsive** — always ready to assist
- **Clear and direct** — avoiding unnecessary jargon
- **Confident yet humble** — knowledgeable without being condescending

---

## Greetings and Closings

### Greetings

| Form | Usage |
|------|-------|
| "Hello" / "Hi" | Standard (most cases) |
| "Good morning/afternoon" | More formal contexts |
| "Dear [Name]" | Formal business correspondence |

### Closings

**Standard:**
- "Best regards"
- "Kind regards"
- "Best"

**Extended:**
- "Please let me know if you have any questions"
- "Happy to help if you need anything else"
- "Looking forward to hearing from you"

### Signature

Every email should end with a professional signature including:
- Name
- Title/Role (if applicable)
- Contact information

---

## Message Structure

### Typical Layout

1. Appropriate greeting
2. Direct acknowledgment of the recipient's message/request
3. Clear, detailed response
4. Next steps or call to action
5. Invitation for follow-up questions
6. Professional closing + signature

---

## Key Style Guidelines

### DO

1. Start with an appropriate greeting
2. Acknowledge the recipient's message
3. Be clear and specific
4. Offer alternatives when declining
5. Respond promptly to questions
6. Confirm next steps
7. Use formatting (lists, paragraphs) for clarity
8. End with a warm closing

### DON'T

1. Use overly casual language in professional contexts
2. Write walls of text without formatting
3. Leave questions unanswered
4. Be dismissive or curt
5. Use excessive jargon
6. Forget to proofread
